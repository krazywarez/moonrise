* concept
An iPhone app for everything in space: what is overhead tonight, what the
sun is doing, what is launching, where our spacecraft are, and a
reference library for the planets, stars, galaxies and missions.

The lead on every tab is a picture computed from real data or a real
image: the sky above you, the solar system today, the sun this hour, a
rocket on its pad. Text sits underneath, a swipe or tap away.

Free, with no purchases. Free of accounts. Works offline for anything
that can be computed; fetches only live feeds.

* design
After NASA's 1975 Graphics Standards Manual.

- SF Pro, the system font (decided 2026-10-21 over Helvetica Neue for
  small-size legibility and Dynamic Type). Tabular figures for all numbers.
- Strict grid, flush left, black hairlines, numbered modules.
- Off-white and ink by day. Near-black with white line work at night.
- One red accent for alerts and emphasis. Not NASA's exact red, no NASA
  insignia or worm logotype, nothing that reads as an official NASA app.
- Drawings are technical: orbits as orbits, dimension lines, to-scale
  comparisons, Gantt-style mission timelines.
- Night mode: red-on-black for dark-adapted eyes, switched on
  automatically after astronomical dusk while the Sky tab is open.
- Motion only where it carries data: planets moving at true relative
  speeds, the moon's phase easing while scrubbing time.

Avoid: neon on black, glow, scan lines, hexagons, crosshairs,
monospaced readouts as decoration.

* screens
Five tabs.

** 1. Sky (home)
- Full-screen sky for your location and time: horizon, stars to about
  magnitude 6, constellation lines, planets, the moon at its true phase,
  the sun below the horizon as a glow at the edge.
- Pans with the device (compass and gyroscope) or by drag. Pinch to zoom.
- Tap any object for a card: name, magnitude, rise and set, distance,
  link to its Library page.
- Time scrubber: drag hours forward or back; tap to return to now.
- Alert chips across the top when relevant: aurora (Kp threshold for
  your latitude), ISS pass within the hour, launch within the hour,
  eclipse or conjunction today.
- Today sheet, pulled up from the bottom:
  - Moon: phase, illumination, next quarter.
  - Visible tonight: planets with direction, altitude, rise and set.
  - ISS passes over the next 3 days.
  - Next launch with countdown.
  - Space weather: Kp now and forecast.
  - Deep space: Voyager 1 distance and light time.

** 2. System
- Top-down orrery of the solar system for today. Log-scaled distances by
  default; toggle to true scale.
- Time slider from 1957 to 2050. Planets and spacecraft move together.
- Spacecraft drawn with their trajectories: Voyager 1 and 2, New
  Horizons, Juno, Europa Clipper, JUICE, Psyche, Parker Solar Probe,
  Lucy, and missions at Mars.
- Tap a planet or spacecraft for its Library page.
- Inset for Earth–Moon space: ISS, JWST at L2, lunar missions.

** 3. Sun
- Latest SDO full-disk image, full width. Swipe between wavelengths:
  171 Å (corona), 304 Å (chromosphere), 193 Å, HMI visible light.
- Kp index: bar chart for the last 3 days and the 3-day forecast.
- Aurora oval for your hemisphere (OVATION model) with your location
  marked.
- Solar flares: GOES X-ray flux for the last 24 hours, flare classes
  marked.
- Plain-language summary in one line: "Quiet", "Minor storm likely
  tonight", and similar.

** 4. Launches
- Upcoming launches, each led by the rocket's silhouette drawn to a
  common scale.
- Detail: vehicle, mission, payload, orbit, pad on a map, countdown,
  launch window, webcast link.
- Live Activity from T−1 hour through orbit insertion.
- Past launches with outcome.

** 5. Library
Every page leads with a drawing or image; facts follow as a spec sheet.

- Solar system: the sun, 8 planets, dwarf planets, major moons. Each has
  a dimensioned drawing, a to-scale comparison with Earth, a facts grid,
  the missions that visited on a timeline, and a short essay.
- Constellations: all 88. Modern line figure, the plate from a
  historical atlas (Bayer, Hevelius, Bode), bright stars, best season,
  where it is tonight.
- Stars: bright named stars with type, distance, colour and the origin
  of the name.
- Deep sky: the 110 Messier objects, then well-known NGC objects, with
  Hubble, Webb or ground images and a scale bar.
- Spacecraft and missions: active and historic. Status, location,
  instruments, findings, timeline.
- Later sections: exoplanets, rockets side by side to scale, the history
  of exploration as a browsable timeline, a scale view from Earth to the
  observable universe.

Search across all of it from the Library tab.

* widgets, Live Activities, notifications
- Lock Screen: moon phase; Kp; next launch countdown.
- Home Screen: tonight's planets; Voyager 1 distance; today's sun image.
- Live Activity: launch countdown through orbit.
- Notifications, each off by default and opt-in: aurora possible at your
  latitude; launch in one hour; ISS visible pass in 15 minutes.

* architecture
- iOS 26+, SwiftUI, Swift 6 language mode. iPhone first; iPad layout in
  v1.1.
- Packages:
  - =SkyEngine=: Swift wrapper over Astronomy Engine's C library.
    Coordinates, rise and set, phases, events, kō-style solar longitude
    (shared with the parked seventy-two app).
  - =SkyRenderer=: SwiftUI Canvas first; move to Metal if 9,000 stars
    plus lines cannot hold 60 fps while panning.
  - =Feeds=: typed clients for SWPC, SDO, CelesTrak, the backend. Cached
    on disk with per-feed lifetimes.
  - =Library=: bundled content (JSON plus images), versioned, updatable
    from the backend without an app release.
- Backend on Cloudflare; see [[*backend][backend]].
- No accounts. Device registers an APNs token and a coarse latitude band
  for aurora alerts, nothing else.

* backend
Only what the app cannot do alone: cache rate-limited feeds (Launch
Library 2, Horizons), send pushes (aurora, launches), drive launch Live
Activities, ship Library content updates. Positions, phases and ISS passes
are computed on device; ISS pass alerts are local notifications. SWPC and
SDO are public, so the app can fetch them directly if the backend is down.

** APNs from Workers
APNs requires HTTP/2. A Cloudflare engineer stated in workerd issue #4841
(2025-08-20) that production requests pass through Cloudflare's proxy,
which upgrades them to HTTP/2; workerd itself, and so =wrangler dev=, does
not support HTTP/2 and there are no plans to add it. Cloudflare's docs do
not state this. Open-source senders rely on it (e.g.
FiveSheepCo/cloudflare-apns2). Containers (Workers Paid) can run an HTTP/2
sender as a fallback. Spike: =spikes/apns-worker=.

- Sign the ES256 provider JWT with WebCrypto; reuse it for 20–60 minutes
  as Apple requires.
- Test pushes against a deployed staging Worker using the sandbox APNs
  host, never from local dev.
- Keep the sender behind the push Queue so it can move to Cloudflare
  Containers (a small Go or Python sender) if Workers' HTTP/2 stops
  working, without touching the rest.
- Delete tokens on HTTP 410.

** alerts
- Aurora: from Kp forecast and OVATION, find the latitude bands that can
  see aurora; notify devices in those bands where it is dark, outside
  quiet hours; at most one per band per storm.
- Launches: "launch in one hour" to opted-in devices; push-to-start the
  Live Activity (iOS 17.2+); update on countdown, hold, liftoff, staging,
  orbit or scrub; end 15 minutes after the outcome.

** privacy and abuse
- Stored per device: push token, 5° latitude band, UTC offset,
  preferences. No location, no accounts.
- v1: per-IP rate limits. Later: App Attest verification in the Worker.

** operations
- Lives in =server/= in this repo (a separate =moonrise-server= repo is
  optional); deploy with Wrangler.
- Staging and production Workers with separate D1 databases and APNs
  environments.
- Workers Logs for errors; feed staleness in =/healthz=.
- Likely Workers Paid plan (about $5/month) plus an optional Launch
  Library 2 key. Check current pricing for Queues and D1 before launch.

* risks
- Library scope: 320 pages is most of the effort. Ship 1.0 with fewer
  pages before adding more.
- Sky render performance and compass accuracy; indoor and urban magnetic
  interference makes device-motion panning unreliable, so drag must
  always work.
- Third-party feed limits (Launch Library 2) and feed outages: show the
  last good data with its age.
- NASA brand rules: the style is borrowed, the branding must not be.
- A crowded category (SkySafari, Night Sky, Stellarium, Next Spaceflight,
  SpaceWeatherLive). The case for this app is all of it in one place,
  designed well, not depth in any one area.

* open questions
- Fallback for APNs if Workers' HTTP/2 stops working: Containers sender
  vs. a separate small VPS. The M0 probe worked, so this is a contingency.
